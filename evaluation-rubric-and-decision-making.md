Evaluation Rubric and Decision-Making

1. Purpose of the Rubric

A structured evaluation rubric helps assess AI-generated responses consistently and fairly.

It provides clear criteria for identifying strengths, weaknesses, and areas that need improvement.

2. The Five Evaluation Criteria

Factual Accuracy

Check whether the information is correct and whether claims are supported by reliable evidence.

Relevance

Determine whether the response directly addresses the user’s request.

Completeness

Check whether the response provides enough valid information to satisfy the task.

Clarity

Assess whether the response is understandable, organized, and easy to follow.

Instruction Following

Verify whether the response follows the requested format, length, tone, and other instructions.

3. My Scoring Approach

* Score each criterion independently.
* Identify specific strengths and weaknesses before assigning scores.
* Do not confuse relevance with factual accuracy.
* Do not penalize factual accuracy simply because formatting instructions were missed.
* Watch for absolute claims such as “always,” “never,” “everyone,” and “guarantees.”
* Explain the reason behind every score.

4. Important Evaluation Lessons

A response can be clear and relevant but still contain misinformation.

A response can be factually accurate but fail to follow the user’s instructions.

A longer response is not automatically better than a shorter one.

One weakness should not automatically reduce every evaluation score.

5. Conclusion

Structured rubric scoring helps AI evaluators make consistent, evidence-based decisions rather than relying on personal opinions.
